# Step 9 — Ten Opportunities a Bangladeshi Startup Could Begin in 12–24 Months

**Selection rule:** a team of 5–20 people could reach first revenue within 12 months and a defensible position within 24, using capital available in Bangladesh today (equity of roughly Tk 2–40 crore, plus debt where the model requires an asset book). Everything requiring legislation, land acquisition, or a government mandate to *start* has been excluded — even where it scored highly in the main ranking.

Ranked by **ease of starting**, not by size of prize. The composite rank below combines the five factors in the table; the individual factor scores are 1–10 where **10 = most favourable to a new entrant**.

## Why each one is startable now

### 1. Land due-diligence reports
**First product:** a standardised, paid property title report combining porcha, mutation records, mouza map and encumbrance checks.
**First customer:** real estate developers and bank credit departments, both of whom currently pay lawyers and intermediaries for this opaquely.
**Why now:** records are partially digitised, so the search is feasible without physical office visits for a growing share of parcels.
**Moat:** the accumulated search dataset plus sub-registrar office process knowledge. Every report makes the next one cheaper — a real, compounding data advantage in a market with none.
**Risk:** liability if a report misses an encumbrance; requires professional indemnity and conservative disclaimers from day one.

### 2. Bid intelligence for contractors
**First product:** a subscription that surfaces relevant tenders, historical award prices, and competitor win patterns from publicly visible e-GP data.
**First customer:** mid-sized civil-works contractors who currently miss tenders.
**Why now:** e-GP has run since 2011, so a decade of award history exists to analyse; and adoption already cuts contract price-to-cost by **10.25–11.85%** [VERIFIED], meaning competitive intelligence has demonstrable value.
**Moat:** the historical award and pricing database — nobody else will have assembled it.
**Risk:** data access could be restricted; build the scrape-and-structure capability defensively and pursue an official data agreement in parallel.

### 3. Pack-house and crate pool
**First product:** one pack-house with grading, washing and pre-cooling, plus a returnable plastic crate rental pool.
**First customer:** a quick-commerce or modern retail buyer with a quality and consistency problem, or an exporter.
**Why now:** modern retail and quick-commerce have finally reached the volume where they will pay for graded, consistent supply; and **20–44% post-harvest loss with USD 2.4bn of annual value destroyed** is the size of the problem being solved. [VERIFIED]
**Moat:** cluster farmer relationships and throughput scale.
**Risk:** throughput risk. Do not build before an offtake contract exists.

### 4. Childcare on a retention business case
**First product:** two or three centres serving one industrial cluster, priced per child per month to employers.
**First customer:** a large RMG group with measurable post-maternity attrition.
**Why now:** **73% of the unemployed female labour force exits after childbirth** and female participation fell from **25.3m to 23.7m in a single year** [VERIFIED] — the problem is measurable, current, and expensive to employers.
**Moat:** safety record and quality reputation, which take years to build and can be destroyed in a day — which is precisely why they are defensible.
**Risk:** a single safety incident. Insurance, protocols and staffing ratios are not optional.

### 5. ODR for lenders
**First product:** a mediation-and-settlement workflow for one MFI's overdue portfolio, priced per resolved case.
**First customer:** a large MFI or MFS lender writing off recoverable balances because litigation is uneconomic.
**Why now:** **4.64 million pending cases** make courts a non-option [VERIFIED], and full payment interoperability (Nov 2025) is about to increase the volume of small digital financial disputes.
**Moat:** settlement outcome data — which disputes settle, at what discount, with which script — becomes a pricing asset competitors cannot replicate.
**Risk:** enforceability without legislation; mitigate with properly drafted contractual mediation clauses.

### 6. Accredited diagnostics network
**First product:** one accredited hub laboratory plus 60–150 franchised collection points in one division, competing on **published** external quality assessment scores and price.
**First customer:** patients directly, plus corporate health check contracts for predictable volume.
**Why now:** reduced access to Indian hospitals has pushed demand onshore [VERIFIED]; and no Bangladeshi lab currently publishes quality data, so the first to do so owns the quality position.
**Moat:** accreditation, brand trust and spoke density.
**Risk:** price competition from unaccredited centres; the answer is corporate and institutional contracts, not consumer price wars.

### 7. Rooftop solar RESCO
**First product:** 5–15 MW across a handful of factories under 15–25 year PPAs at zero capex to the host.
**First customer:** one large RMG group with multiple factories — one relationship, many roofs.
**Why now:** the **Net Metering Guidelines 2025** raised eligibility from 70% to 100% of sanctioned load and opened the scheme to single-phase consumers; a **December 2025 circular made rooftop solar mandatory for new connections on buildings with ≥1,000 sq ft of usable roof**; and only **~116 MW is installed against ~5,000 MW of identified industrial rooftop potential**. [VERIFIED]
**Moat:** the PPAs themselves — 25 years of contracted cash flow — plus access to long-tenor capital, which is the real competitive variable.
**Risk:** offtaker credit. Underwrite the factory, not the roof.

### 8. Supply-chain finance
**First product:** working capital for the dealer network of one large FMCG, pharmaceutical or electronics distributor, underwritten on verified purchase history from the anchor.
**First customer:** the distributor, not the dealers — the anchor is the sale.
**Why now:** the **USD 2.8bn MSME financing gap** with only **~28% of SMEs accessing formal credit** [VERIFIED], and Bangla QR merchant digitisation creating verifiable revenue histories.
**Moat:** anchor relationships plus a repayment dataset that lowers funding cost over time.
**Risk:** credit and funding cost; start small, prove PAR30 below 5%, then raise wholesale debt.

### 9. PAYGO three-wheeler finance
**First product:** finance 500–1,000 electric three-wheelers in one district with battery-as-a-service and daily MFS repayment.
**First customer:** drivers currently renting daily from vehicle owners.
**Why now:** M-KOPA's Kenyan precedent (**USD 1.6bn disbursed, 4.8m customers, 5,000+ e-motorbikes**) [VERIFIED], plus **239.3m MFS accounts** making daily micro-collection viable, plus falling lithium costs making a safe alternative to informal lead-acid economic.
**Moat:** the repayment dataset, MFI distribution partnerships, and battery swap density.
**Risk:** **the highest regulatory risk on this list** — electric three-wheelers are periodically banned or restricted. This risk must be priced explicitly and the business geographically diversified.

### 10. Care-worker academy for Japan/Korea
**First product:** language plus care skills training for one destination market with one employer partner.
**First customer:** a Japanese or Korean care home group, or a German state health authority — the **employer pays**, not the trainee.
**Why now:** Japan and Korea have created formal care-worker visa categories driven by demographics that will not reverse; and Bangladesh currently captures almost none of this flow.
**Moat:** destination employer relationships and publicly verifiable licensure pass rates.
**Risk:** longest time to revenue on this list (18–24 months, because language training takes that long) — this is the one item here requiring genuine patience capital.

## What a Bangladeshi startup should *not* start

Three of the highest-scoring opportunities in the main ranking are **poor startup candidates** and it is worth saying so explicitly:

- **#4 VAT e-invoicing** — the platform is a government mandate. A startup should build the *provider* layer (invoicing SaaS for mid-sized firms) and wait to be certified.
- **#8 Health purchasing** — the purchaser must be public. A startup should build **claims and TPA technology** for the private and corporate health market, which exists today.
- **#45 Rural commerce / B2B retail** — ShopUp has raised **~USD 137m** including a **USD 62.89m Series B4** [VERIFIED]. Competing head-on would violate this dossier's own Rule 7. Enter on the **production** side instead.

## The pattern worth noticing

Nine of these ten start by selling to an **institution that already bears the cost** — a developer paying for title risk, a contractor paying for lost tenders, a retailer paying for spoilage, a factory paying for turnover, a lender paying for uncollected debt, a hospital paying for unreliable results, a factory paying for electricity, a distributor paying for dealer credit, a fleet owner paying for battery replacement. None of them requires creating new demand.

**In a market where consumers are price-sensitive and institutions are cost-blind, the fastest route to revenue is to find a cost someone is already paying, and charge less for it.**
