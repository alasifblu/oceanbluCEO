# Step 10 — Opportunities That Could Become Export Industries

The test applied here is stricter than "could be sold abroad". It is: **could Bangladesh develop the capability domestically, at a cost and quality level that other emerging markets would buy, within roughly a decade?** That requires a genuine Bangladeshi advantage — frugal cost base, scale of the domestic problem, an existing labour or institutional asset — not merely competence.

Bangladesh's exportable advantages, honestly stated:

| Advantage | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Very large, young, low-cost labour force** | 77.4m labour force [VERIFIED] |
| **Frugal-innovation track record at population scale** | BRAC, Grameen, the solar home systems programme, oral rehydration, community clinics — Bangladesh has repeatedly built the world's largest version of a low-cost delivery model |
| **Extreme problem severity** | Delta geography, density, climate exposure — solutions that work here are over-engineered for most other markets |
| **A globally significant apparel industry** | 2nd-largest apparel exporter; ~400,000 t/yr of clean post-industrial textile waste [VERIFIED] |
| **A large diaspora as a beachhead market** | USD 32.8bn/yr remittance flow [VERIFIED] |
| **Institutional innovations nobody has productised** | The Accord/RSC factory safety governance model: **1,600+ factories covered; >1,200 completed >90% of corrective action plans** [VERIFIED] |

## Tier 1 — Strongest export potential

### E1. IT services and global capability centres (#48)
**What is exported:** software engineering, analytics, finance and back-office services.
**Bangladesh's advantage:** the largest untapped young technical workforce in Asia at its wage level, now materially cheaper than India or Vietnam for equivalent skills, and with proven delivery relationships in specific markets (Cefalo–Norway, BJIT–Japan, Therap–US healthcare).
**Target export markets:** Nordics, Japan, Australia, the Gulf, and the UK — deliberately *not* the saturated US mid-market where Indian competition is strongest.
**Scale of the prize:** the Philippines built **USD 38bn of revenue and 1.82m jobs, ~8% of GDP** from an economy with similar structural characteristics. [VERIFIED] Bangladesh currently exports **USD 724.6m**. Even a quarter of the Philippine scale is USD 9.5bn/yr.
**What must be true:** English-language capability at industrial scale; guaranteed connectivity with a binding no-shutdown commitment; and two or three anchor multinational centres as proof.
**Honest risk:** country reputation and internet reliability are the binding constraints, and both are political rather than commercial.

### E2. Care and health workforce (#31)
**What is exported:** trained, language-capable nurses and long-term care workers.
**Bangladesh's advantage:** a very large young female workforce, a nursing education base that grew **421% in 15 years** [VERIFIED], and — uniquely — a *domestic* shortage of **~82%** that makes expanded training defensible at home as well as abroad.
**Target export markets:** Japan, Korea, Germany, the UK, and the Gulf — all facing structural, demographically-driven care shortages that will persist for decades.
**Scale of the prize:** 20,000 placements/yr generating **Tk 14.4–24bn/yr of remittance from a single cohort**, compounding as cohorts accumulate. [CALCULATED]
**What must be true:** language training at destination-standard quality; mutual recognition of qualifications; and employer-funded rather than worker-funded placement.
**Honest risk:** the Philippines, India and Nepal are years ahead; Bangladesh competes on training quality and ethical recruitment, not on being first.

### E3. Circular apparel and textile recycling (#50)
**What is exported:** recycled fibre and yarn, certified recycled-content garments, and — the higher-value layer — **traceability and certification services**.
**Bangladesh's advantage:** unmatched feedstock. **~400,000 t/yr** of clean, single-composition, geographically concentrated post-industrial textile waste; the world's second-largest apparel manufacturing base to consume the output; and existing spinning capacity. [VERIFIED]
**Target export markets:** EU and US brands facing circularity regulation, which is arriving on a legislated timetable this decade.
**Scale of the prize:** **~USD 3bn** market potential and **USD 500–700m/yr** of import saving. [VERIFIED]
**What must be true:** bonded-warehouse and customs rules must permit waste to move from a bonded factory to a domestic recycler — currently the rules effectively favour exporting the waste.
**Honest risk:** mechanical recycling only. Renewcell's 2024 bankruptcy is the warning against capital-intensive chemical recycling without secured offtake.

## Tier 2 — Credible with sustained investment

### E4. Frugal climate-adaptation systems for delta economies (#27, #24, #46)
**What is exported:** integrated packages for saline agriculture, flood-tolerant production systems, solar irrigation service models, and parametric flood insurance — designed and proven at scale in the world's most exposed delta.
**Bangladesh's advantage:** nobody has a harder testing ground. A system that works in the Khulna coastal belt is over-specified for the Mekong, the Niger, the Nile or the Irrawaddy.
**Target export markets:** Vietnam, Myanmar, Egypt, Nigeria, Mozambique, the Philippines, Indonesia — and increasingly, the climate-finance institutions that fund adaptation in all of them.
**Scale of the prize:** the export is the *method and the service*, not commodities — consultancy, implementation partnership, and licensed product design.
**What must be true:** Bangladesh must first build a commercially self-sustaining domestic adaptation delivery business (the whole point of Opp 27's design). Grant-funded pilots cannot be exported; businesses can.
**Honest risk:** this is a services export with a long sales cycle and low margins early.

### E5. Factory compliance and social auditing services (from gap 107, #20, #50)
**What is exported:** compliance systems, remediation methodology, chain-of-custody traceability, and auditing capability.
**Bangladesh's advantage:** the **Accord/RSC** is a genuine Bangladeshi institutional innovation — a tripartite, brand-financed, publicly-disclosing factory safety governance system covering **1,600+ factories**, with **>1,200 factories having completed >90% of their corrective action plans**. [VERIFIED] It was created here, under the worst possible circumstances, and it worked. **No one has productised it.**
**Target export markets:** apparel and light manufacturing sectors in Vietnam, Cambodia, Ethiopia, Egypt, Myanmar, Pakistan and India, all of which face the same buyer due-diligence pressure Bangladesh faced in 2013.
**Scale of the prize:** a services business, not a large one in revenue terms — but strategically significant, because it converts Bangladesh's most painful episode into an exportable competence.
**What must be true:** the RSC's methodology would need to be codified and commercialised by a separate entity, with the tripartite governance model as the product.
**Honest risk:** this is the least conventional item in this file and would need a champion; it is included because it is the clearest example of an asset Bangladesh already owns and has never monetised.

### E6. Low-cost tropical protected cultivation (#22)
**What is exported:** greenhouse and shade-net structure designs, fabrication, and cluster agronomy models engineered for heat, humidity and cyclone loads at South Asian cost levels.
**Bangladesh's advantage:** if Bangladesh solves protected cultivation for its own climate, the design is directly transferable across tropical Asia and Africa — and Bangladeshi light engineering can fabricate at a cost European and Israeli suppliers cannot match.
**Target export markets:** Myanmar, Nepal, Sri Lanka, East and West Africa.
**What must be true:** a domestic cluster must work first. Nothing is exportable until it is proven at home.

### E7. Ship recycling and hazardous materials management (#49, gap 91)
**What is exported:** compliant ship recycling services (already an export) plus, potentially, downstream hazardous waste handling capability.
**Bangladesh's advantage:** **17 IMO-notified yards — second globally after Türkiye's 18 — and 23 yards classified green**, with the Hong Kong Convention in force since **26 June 2025**. [VERIFIED]
**Target export markets:** global shipowners requiring HKC-compliant recycling.
**What must be true:** the downstream hazardous waste and worker safety record must become genuinely credible; "green certification alone cannot guarantee safe ship recycling" is the sector's own honest assessment.
**Honest risk:** the sector's safety record is the reputational constraint, and worker deaths continue to be reported. This is an export industry Bangladesh already has and could lose.

## The strategic point about exports

Bangladesh's export conversation is almost entirely about **goods**, and almost entirely about apparel. Every item in Tier 1 of this file is either a **service** or a **capability**, and services face **no tariffs** — which means LDC graduation on **24 November 2026**, which will damage goods exports by up to **USD 8bn/yr** [VERIFIED], does **not** damage them at all.

That is the single most important asymmetry in Bangladesh's 2026–2035 export strategy, and it is under-discussed: **the graduation shock falls almost entirely on the export category Bangladesh is over-concentrated in, and not at all on the categories it has barely entered.**
