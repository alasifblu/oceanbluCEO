# Source & Evidence Index

Every source below was retrieved during this research cycle (September 2026). Sources are grouped by what they evidence. **Where a figure is contested, ranged, or came from secondary reporting of a primary study, that is stated in the "What it proves" column** — this index is intended to let a reader challenge the dossier, not to make it look better-sourced than it is.

**Convention:** "*(secondary)*" marks reputable journalism reporting a primary study; the primary study should be obtained before any figure is used in a board paper or a government submission.

## Known weaknesses in this evidence base

Stated explicitly so that a reviewer can direct verification effort where it matters:

1. **Several headline Bangladeshi figures reach this dossier through journalism reporting a primary study.** The most important cases — the World Bank lead study (source 60), the BIDS out-of-pocket figure (33), the BUET congestion estimate (86), and the USD 5bn medical tourism figure (47) — should each be traced to the underlying report before external use. Each is flagged *(secondary)* above.

2. **The Dhaka congestion cost estimates vary by more than an order of magnitude** across sources (Tk 370bn/yr to USD 3–5bn/yr, and 5m to 19m work hours). The dossier treats this as an order-of-magnitude problem rather than a number, which is the honest reading.

3. **The Bangladesh e-waste tonnage (~2.81–3 million t/yr) is implausibly high** relative to the country's electronics consumption base and probably includes ship-breaking and industrial streams. The dossier therefore relies on the **97% informal / 3% formal split** instead, which is more decision-relevant and less likely to be wrong.

4. **The Chittagong port CPPI rank (341/370) is from the 2021 index.** It should be refreshed against the current edition (source 94) before publication.

5. **Two opportunities have materially weaker international evidence than the rest**: planned migrant-receiving secondary cities (#19) and saline-adaptive coastal systems (#27). Both rely on programme existence and policy adoption rather than on quantified outcome evaluations. This is stated in their own files and is reflected in their International Proof scores (5 and 6).

6. **Two sources are commercial parties to the market they describe**: TOMRA (71, 73) sells deposit-return equipment, and M-KOPA (125) publishes its own impact data. Both are corroborated where possible (SINTEF at 72; TechCabal at 124), but their outcome claims should be treated as self-reported.

7. **Several Bangladesh "current situation" classifications rest on the absence of a search result rather than on a confirmed negative.** Every such case is marked [ASSUMPTION] in the opportunity files with the specific agency to verify with. Before any capital commitment, these must be confirmed with the named agency — an opportunity that turns out to be already served is the most expensive error this kind of research can make, and source 146 (ShopUp) is the example of it being caught.
