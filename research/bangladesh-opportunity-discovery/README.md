# Bangladesh Opportunity Discovery
### 50 proven international models that could transform Bangladesh, 2026–2035

**Prepared for:** investment committee / national innovation strategy review
**Prepared by:** OceanBlu strategy research
**Date of research:** September 2026
**Evidence window:** primarily 2020–2026 sources; older sources used only to document the original implementation of an established model.

---

## How to read this dossier

This is **not** an idea list. It is a structured discovery exercise that works backwards:

> **Bangladesh problem → existing international solution → evidence it works → Bangladesh gap → localization → business/government model → feasibility → opportunity**

Every opportunity was required to clear four gates before inclusion:

| Gate | Test |
|---|---|
| **1. Real problem** | A documented Bangladeshi problem with a measurable population or economic loss attached, sourced. |
| **2. Proven elsewhere** | A named country, named organisation, named year, and at least one quantitative outcome from a credible source (multilateral, government, peer-reviewed, or established trade press). |
| **3. Genuine Bangladesh gap** | An explicit search for existing Bangladeshi implementations, classified **Absent / Early-stage / Fragmented / Underperforming / Mature**. Anything found to be **Mature** was rejected unless a fundamentally different model applies. |
| **4. Path to self-financing** | A revenue or public-finance model that does not depend permanently on grants. |

### Evidence labelling convention used throughout

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **[VERIFIED]** | Attributable to a named source in the Source & Evidence Index. |
| **[CALCULATED]** | Arithmetic derived from verified inputs; the derivation is shown. |
| **[ASSUMPTION]** | A judgement call with no direct source. Treat as a hypothesis to be tested, not a fact. |

Market sizes are given in **BDT** with USD in brackets at **BDT 122 = USD 1** [ASSUMPTION — working rate for FY2025-26 planning; actual rate varies].

## The one-paragraph thesis

Bangladesh's binding constraint is **no longer capital, labour, or even electricity — it is the absence of trustworthy shared infrastructure**: registries that can be relied on, data that can move between institutions, quality that can be certified, risk that can be priced, and skills that can be proved. The country built a world-class *volume* economy (garments, remittance, rice, mobile money) on top of institutions that cannot verify anything. That is why 4.6 million cases sit in court, why MSMEs face a USD 2.8bn credit gap despite 239 million mobile-money accounts, why leather sells to China at a discount instead of to Europe at a premium, why migrants pay USD 3,900 to earn USD 200 a month, and why families spend USD 5bn a year on hospitals in other countries. **The highest-return imports for Bangladesh over 2026–2035 are not products. They are verification systems** — and almost every one of them has a working, financially self-sustaining precedent in a country poorer or only slightly richer than Bangladesh.

The full argument is in [`13-final-output.md`](13-final-output.md), Section G.
