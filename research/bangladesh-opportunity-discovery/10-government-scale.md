# Step 8 — Ten Government-Scale Transformation Opportunities

Selected for **national impact if adopted by the Government of Bangladesh**, not for commercial return. Each entry names the responsible agency, the problem, the precedent, indicative cost and benefit, PPP suitability, and a pilot-to-rollout path.

Costs are **[ASSUMPTION]** planning ranges unless marked otherwise; benefits are **[CALCULATED]** from **[VERIFIED]** inputs where possible. None should be treated as a budget.

---

## G1 — Mandatory VAT e-invoicing and revenue analytics (#4)
| | |
|---|---|
| **Ministry/agency** | National Board of Revenue (VAT Wing), Internal Resources Division |
| **National problem** | Tax/GDP fell to **6.56% in FY25** from 7.2%; NBR missed target by **Tk 92,625 crore**; collection was 3.08% *below* the prior year. [VERIFIED] |
| **Precedent** | Brazil NF-e (2008), India GST e-invoicing (2020), Türkiye e-Fatura (2012), Chile, Mexico, Italy |
| **Indicative cost** | Tk 400–900 crore for platform, analytics and rollout over 3 years |
| **Indicative benefit** | Each percentage point of tax/GDP ≈ **USD 5bn/yr** on a ~USD 500bn economy. NBR's own FY35 target of 10.5% implies ~USD 20bn/yr. [CALCULATED] |
| **PPP** | Yes — government platform, certified private providers above the API |
| **Pilot** | Top 1,000–2,000 VAT payers, national, by turnover threshold |
| **Rollout** | Threshold lowered annually on a published schedule; free government invoicing app for small firms; consent-based data sharing to lenders as the incentive to comply |

**Why this is first.** Every other item on this list is fiscally constrained. This one relaxes the constraint.

## G2 — Conclusive land titling with a state-backed indemnity fund (#2)
| | |
|---|---|
| **Ministry/agency** | Ministry of Land; Directorate of Land Records and Surveys; Law and Justice Division |
| **National problem** | ~**80% of rural court cases** are land disputes; **>4 million land cases pending**. [VERIFIED] Land cannot be reliably pledged, sold or inherited. |
| **Precedent** | Rwanda LTR (2009–13), India SVAMITVA (2020), Estonia and Georgia registry-and-guarantee models |
| **Indicative cost** | Tk 30–80bn for national survey, digitisation and systems over 5–8 years |
| **Indicative benefit** | Authorities themselves expect a **30% reduction in land litigation within five years** [VERIFIED as an official expectation]; plus the creation of a bankable collateral class |
| **PPP** | Yes for survey, systems and title insurance; adjudication and guarantee must be public |
| **Pilot** | Feni (building on the existing UNDP-supported pilot) plus one peri-urban high-value upazila |
| **Rollout** | District-by-district declaration of conclusive title after a publicised objection window; indemnity fund financed by a registration levy so the system funds its own risk |

## G3 — A strategic health purchasing agency (#8)
| | |
|---|---|
| **Ministry/agency** | MoHFW; Health Economics Unit; a new statutory purchasing authority |
| **National problem** | Out-of-pocket spending **~79% (2024)**, up from 73% (2021); poorest households spend **~35% of income** on health. [VERIFIED] |
| **Precedent** | Thailand UCS (2001–02), India PM-JAY (2018), Indonesia JKN (2014) — **and Bangladesh's own SSK** |
| **Indicative cost** | **Tk 10bn/yr** to cover 10 million poorest households at SSK's own Tk 1,000 premium; **Tk 25–40bn/yr** at a more realistic premium [CALCULATED from VERIFIED SSK unit economics] |
| **Indicative benefit** | SSK's own evaluation: **92% lower out-of-pocket expenditure** and **72% lower catastrophic health expenditure** among inpatient users. [VERIFIED] Plus retention of part of the **USD ~5bn/yr** spent on treatment abroad |
| **PPP** | Yes on claims administration and provider networks; purchasing itself must be public |
| **Pilot** | Scale SSK from three Tangail upazilas to a full district **with private provider empanelment added** |
| **Rollout** | Division by division, package widened as fiscal space from G1 materialises |

**The decisive fact:** Bangladesh has already proved this works on its own population and then did not scale it.

## G4 — Government-to-government zero-fee migration corridors (#7)
| | |
|---|---|
| **Ministry/agency** | MoEWOE, BMET, BOESL, Ministry of Foreign Affairs, NSDA |
| **National problem** | Average migration cost **Tk 416,789** against average overseas income **Tk 23,693** — **17+ months** to break even; Singapore corridor **Tk 574,241**; average recruitment cost ~**USD 3,900** against a G2G-Plus design target of **USD 420**. [VERIFIED] |
| **Precedent** | Korea's Employment Permit System (2004), with MOUs with 15 Asian origin countries including Bangladesh |
| **Indicative cost** | Tk 300–600 crore for testing, certification and training infrastructure |
| **Indicative benefit** | Korea's EPS cut recruitment cost from **USD 3,509 (2001) to USD 941 (2014)**. [VERIFIED] Applying a comparable reduction to 200,000 Bangladeshi workers/yr returns **~Tk 70bn/yr directly to migrant households**. [CALCULATED] |
| **PPP** | Essential — the state cannot run employer relationships at scale |
| **Pilot** | One corridor (Japan SSW or expanded Korea EPS), 1,000–3,000 workers, from Cumilla/Brahmanbaria/Chattogram |
| **Rollout** | Employer-pays clauses mandatory in all new bilateral MOUs; agency licences tied to verified fee compliance; a published corridor price index as the enforcement instrument |

## G5 — A national data exchange layer (#1)
| | |
|---|---|
| **Ministry/agency** | ICT Division; Bangladesh Computer Council; Bangladesh Bank for financial queries |
| **National problem** | NID, land, tax, health, e-GP and social protection systems are silos; no once-only principle; benefits cannot be deduplicated; SME lending cannot see tax or utility data |
| **Precedent** | Estonia X-Road (2001), Rwanda IremboGov (2015), India Account Aggregator (2021) |
| **Indicative cost** | Tk 200–500 crore for the core plus 20–30 agency integrations |
| **Indicative benefit** | Estonia: **>820 years of working time saved annually**, ~**2% of GDP** attributed to digital signatures alone; Rwanda: **>100 million working hours saved**, **>25m applications**, ~**USD 300m** transacted, **self-financed by commission**. [VERIFIED] |
| **PPP** | Yes — Irembo is the template, including its commission-based sustainability |
| **Pilot** | Three agencies, two banks, one district, ~50,000 transactions |
| **Rollout** | Corridor by corridor, starting with NID↔land, NID↔education, NID↔trade licence; a Once-Only Act to make it binding |

## G6 — Bus route franchising for Dhaka and Chattogram (#11)
| | |
|---|---|
| **Ministry/agency** | DTCA, Dhaka North/South and Chattogram City Corporations, BRTA, MoRTB |
| **National problem** | Congestion costs estimated between **Tk 370bn/yr** and **USD 3–5bn/yr**; ~**5m work hours** lost annually; **~102 road deaths per 10,000 vehicles** — South Asia's worst, against India's 13 — with crash costs up to **5.1% of GDP**. [VERIFIED] |
| **Precedent** | Bogotá TransMilenio (2000): **32% travel time reduction**, **44% SO₂ reduction**, **15–20% property value uplift**, at **USD 5.4–5.9m/km** for Phase I. [VERIFIED] |
| **Indicative cost** | Tk 1,500–4,000 crore for corridor infrastructure and fare systems across several bundles; far less if buses are operator-financed |
| **Indicative benefit** | Applying Bogotá's travel-time result to Dhaka's congestion cost implies benefits in the **tens of billions of taka annually** [CALCULATED, order of magnitude] |
| **PPP** | Essential |
| **Pilot** | One corridor bundle (Mirpur–Motijheel), 150,000–300,000 daily passengers |
| **Rollout** | Bundle by bundle; the pivotal legal change is amending route-permit law to allow **company-only** permits |

## G7 — Foundational literacy and numeracy at scale (#29)
| | |
|---|---|
| **Ministry/agency** | Directorate of Primary Education; MoPME; NCTB; NAPE |
| **National problem** | Learning poverty **51–57%** despite **~98% enrolment**; learning poverty **35.2 points higher** than income poverty. [VERIFIED] |
| **Precedent** | Pratham TaRL (India), Zambia Catch Up (2016), Kenya Tusome (2015) — among the most-replicated randomised results in development economics |
| **Indicative cost** | **Tk 7.2–18bn/yr** for national coverage of ~18–20m primary children at Tk 400–900/child [CALCULATED] |
| **Indicative benefit** | Not monetisable in-year. The counterfactual is that the majority of a 175-million-person country's human capital formation continues to fail. |
| **PPP** | Yes, in outcome-contracted form, with an implementer such as BRAC |
| **Pilot** | 200–400 government primary schools, two upazilas, with a randomised evaluation |
| **Rollout** | Upazila by upazila with published learning results; the pivotal regulatory change is permission to group children **by level** for a defined daily period |

## G8 — Battery and electronics extended producer responsibility (#49)
| | |
|---|---|
| **Ministry/agency** | Department of Environment; MoEFCC; Ministry of Industries; Bangladesh Ship Recycling Board |
| **National problem** | **~28.5m children** with blood lead above 5 µg/dL; **~20.6m IQ points** lost among under-fives; estimated cost **USD 10.9bn ≈ 3.6% of GDP**; **1,100+ informal ULAB operations**; **97% of e-waste** handled informally against 3% formal. [VERIFIED] |
| **Precedent** | India's Battery Waste Management Rules (2022); Umicore (Belgium); UNEP/Pure Earth ULAB formalisation programmes |
| **Indicative cost** | Minimal to the Treasury — the levy is paid by producers. Enforcement and site remediation: Tk 100–300 crore |
| **Indicative benefit** | Even a **10% reduction** in the estimated USD 10.9bn annual cost is **USD 1.09bn/yr**. [CALCULATED] The IQ loss is permanent and non-recoverable, which makes delay uniquely expensive. |
| **PPP** | Yes — producer-funded, privately operated, publicly regulated |
| **Pilot** | One compliant plant plus a collection network paying above informal prices; blood-lead screening in affected communities |
| **Rollout** | Operationalise the stalled **Hazardous Waste (e-waste) Management Rules 2021** using TIB's critique as the fix list; levy on batteries and electronics; enforcement against the largest informal smelters only *after* a formal price channel exists |

## G9 — A national skills credit with published provider outcomes (#10 + #35)
| | |
|---|---|
| **Ministry/agency** | NSDA; Ministry of Education (TVET); Ministry of Labour; MoEWOE |
| **National problem** | Supply-driven TVET with distrusted certificates; LDC graduation on **24 Nov 2026** exposes up to **USD 8bn of export earnings** and a modelled **11.8% RMG export decline** in a sector employing ~4m people. [VERIFIED] |
| **Precedent** | Singapore SkillsFuture (2015–16): **~1.05m users (37% of population)**, **69% reporting improved work performance**; Indonesia Kartu Prakerja (2020): significant employment gains **but insignificant in rural areas** — the key design warning. [VERIFIED] |
| **Indicative cost** | **Tk 58bn/yr** for a Tk 15,000 credit to 5% of the labour force; a first phase at 500,000 workers costs **Tk 7.5bn/yr** [CALCULATED] |
| **Indicative benefit** | Wage gains via better matching; a national labour market information system as a by-product; and the first real quality signal in Bangladeshi TVET |
| **PPP** | Yes — government-funded, privately delivered marketplace |
| **Pilot** | 50,000–100,000 workers in the Gazipur/Narayanganj/Savar RMG belt, with a randomised design |
| **Rollout** | Occupation by occupation, restricted to roles with verified employer demand; **the durable product is the published per-provider outcome table**, not the credit itself |

## G10 — Common effluent treatment as a performance-contracted utility (#20)
| | |
|---|---|
| **Ministry/agency** | Ministry of Industries; BSCIC; Department of Environment; EPB |
| **National problem** | Savar's CETP "continues to release untreated water"; **300 of 1,710 LWG certification points** relate to the CETP; only **~8 LWG-certified entities**; ~**140 of 162 tanneries** operating but selling raw leather to China at a discount; the sector is **stuck near USD 1bn against USD 5bn potential**. [VERIFIED] |
| **Precedent** | Consorzio Aquarno, Santa Croce sull'Arno (Italy) — an industry-owned tannery effluent consortium; Tamil Nadu CETPs; Türkiye's OIZ shared-utility model |
| **Indicative cost** | Tk 40–90 crore for rehabilitation and one year of contracted operations; substantially more for a new plant |
| **Indicative benefit** | The gap between **USD 1bn and USD 5bn** of leather exports is, functionally, the value of a working treatment plant — **USD 4bn (Tk 488bn)**. [VERIFIED figures; the causal attribution is a judgement] |
| **PPP** | Essential — the state has demonstrated it cannot operate this asset |
| **Pilot** | Savar Tannery Industrial Estate, 140 operating tanneries |
| **Rollout** | Replicate the performance-contract model at other industrial estates; the pivotal reform is **paying per m³ treated to standard with published continuous online monitoring**, not building more capital |

## Sequencing: what a government should do in what order

| Phase | Items | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Year 1 (regulatory, near-zero cost)** | G8 (EPR levy), G10 (CETP contract restructure), the G9 outcome-publication rule, e-GP open data (#6), off-site childcare recognition (#34), electric three-wheeler standards (#43) | These are **rule changes, not budgets**. They unlock private capital without fiscal space. |
| **Years 1–3 (fiscal foundation)** | G1 (e-invoicing), G5 (data exchange) | Everything below depends on revenue and on data that can move. |
| **Years 2–5 (institution building)** | G2 (land titling), G3 (health purchasing), G4 (migration corridors), G7 (foundational literacy) | High-severity, low-feasibility items requiring legislation and sustained political sponsorship. Start pilots now so the evidence exists when the fiscal space arrives. |
| **Years 3–8 (capital programmes)** | G6 (bus franchising), plus the infrastructure items in file 03 | Capital-intensive and politically hardest; sequence after the revenue base and the institutional capability exist. |

**The single most important observation:** the Year 1 column costs the Treasury almost nothing and could plausibly unlock more private investment than the entire capital programme. Bangladesh's binding constraint on these opportunities is not money — it is **six or seven specific rule changes that no one has made**.
