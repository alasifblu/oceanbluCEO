# Steps 1–4 — Method, Screening, and the Bangladesh Gap Map

---

## 1. What this exercise did, in order

1. **Mapped Bangladesh's structural gaps first** (112 catalogued below), deliberately before considering any solution, to avoid the common failure mode of starting from fashionable technology and reverse-engineering a problem.
2. **Searched internationally** for models that address each gap, weighting *transferable* precedents (India, Vietnam, Indonesia, Kenya, Rwanda, Brazil, Colombia, Turkey, China, Philippines, Thailand) above rich-country precedents, because cost structure and state capacity — not sophistication — determine transferability.
3. **Verified the international model** against multilateral, government, peer-reviewed or established-trade-press sources, requiring at minimum: years in operation, a scale metric, and a financing model.
4. **Verified Bangladesh's current state** by explicitly searching for existing Bangladeshi startups, government projects, NGO programmes, donor projects and PPPs before asserting any gap.
5. **Scored and ranked** on ten weighted criteria (Step 6).

### Screening rules applied

- **Rejected on sight:** "AI chatbot for business", generic food-delivery, generic marketplace, "another MFS wallet", "another ride-hailing app". Roughly 40 candidate ideas were discarded under Rule 7.
- **Rejected as Mature:** mobile financial services (239.3m accounts, Tk 1.72 trillion/month as of Jan 2025 — this is a mature market, not an opportunity) [VERIFIED]; microcredit for household consumption; basic mobile top-up; SIM-based remittance receipt; primary school *enrolment* (98% — the gap is learning, not access) [VERIFIED]; basic tubewell water access (97% "improved source" coverage — the gap is *safety*, not access) [VERIFIED]. In each case the residual opportunity was reframed onto the part of the system that is genuinely broken.
- **Rejected as unfinanceable:** several climate-adaptation and disability-inclusion concepts with real social value but no path off permanent grant dependence. Where possible these were re-cast with a cross-subsidy or B2G procurement model and retained; where not, they were dropped.

## 3. From 112 gaps to 50 opportunities

The 112 gaps collapse into **eight repeating root causes**. This is the analytical heart of the exercise and it drives the strategic conclusion in `13-final-output.md`:

| Root cause | Gaps it explains | What it means commercially |
