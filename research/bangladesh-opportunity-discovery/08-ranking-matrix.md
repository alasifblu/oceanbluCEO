# Step 6 — Ranking Matrix: all 50 opportunities scored and tiered

## Method

Each opportunity was scored **1–10** on ten criteria and combined into a weighted score. Scores are analyst judgements informed by the evidence in files 02–07; they are **not** measurements, and two reasonable analysts would differ by roughly ±0.5 on many rows. The value of the matrix is the *relative ordering and the visible reasoning*, not the second decimal place.

| Criterion | Weight | What a 10 means |
|---|---:|---|
| **Bangladesh Problem Severity** (Sev) | 15% | Affects tens of millions or costs percentage points of GDP, with sourced evidence |
| **Market Potential** (Mkt) | 15% | Multi-billion-dollar addressable value with a real payer |
| **International Proof** (Proof) | 10% | Multiple countries, years of operation, quantified outcomes from credible sources |
| **Bangladesh Gap** (Gap) | 10% | Genuinely absent or badly underperforming; no well-funded incumbent |
| **Financial Sustainability** (Fin) | 10% | Self-financing from revenue or recurring public procurement, no permanent grant dependence |
| **Implementation Feasibility** (Feas) | 10% | Can be delivered without legislative change, land acquisition, or political consensus |
| **Scalability** (Scale) | 10% | The model gets cheaper per unit as it grows and can reach national scale |
| **Social / Economic Impact** (Impact) | 10% | Large, measurable effects on income, health, employment or fiscal position |
| **Competitive Advantage** (Adv) | 5% | A durable moat is available to an early mover |
| **2026–2030 Timing** (Time) | 5% | A dated catalyst makes now materially better than five years ago or five years hence |

**Note on the feasibility weighting.** Feasibility is only 10%, which is deliberate: this dossier is for a strategy discussion, not a next-quarter plan, and several of the highest-value opportunities (land titling, health purchasing, bus franchising) are scored 2–3 on feasibility precisely because they are hard. A reader whose mandate is "what can we start in 12 months" should read **file 11 (startup-scale)** rather than this ranking, which deliberately does not penalise national-scale institutional reform for being difficult.

## Reading the results: five things the matrix reveals

### 1. The top of the table is dominated by opportunities where the enabling event has *just happened*
Four of the top six — cash-flow SME lending, rooftop solar, e-invoicing and PAYGO asset finance — depend on regulatory or infrastructure changes that occurred between **November 2024 and December 2025**: full payments interoperability (1 Nov 2025), the Net Metering Guidelines 2025 and the December 2025 mandatory-rooftop circular, and the post-2024 fiscal pressure that has put revenue reform on the agenda. This is not a coincidence of scoring; it reflects that **Bangladesh's opportunity set changed materially in the last 18 months** and most market participants have not repriced it yet.

### 2. Feasibility and impact are strongly negatively correlated
The three opportunities scoring 10 on Bangladesh Problem Severity — land titling (#2), e-invoicing (#4), migration corridors (#7), health purchasing (#8), foundational literacy (#29) and lead recycling (#49) — score an average of **3.3 on feasibility**. The three highest-feasibility opportunities — diagnostics (#36, 7), rooftop solar (#17, 7) and blood supply (#42, 7) — average **7.7 on severity**. There is no free lunch in this list, and any strategy that only picks the easy items will systematically avoid the country's largest problems.

### 3. The private sector can only capture a fraction of the value it creates in the highest-scoring items
E-invoicing (#4) could add several percentage points of GDP in tax revenue; the private provider layer captures perhaps **Tk 1–6bn/yr**. Migration corridors (#7) could return **Tk 70bn/yr** to migrant households; the ethical recruitment operator captures a fee. This is a systematic feature of institutional opportunities and it explains why they remain unaddressed: **the social return massively exceeds the private return**, so nobody builds them without a public mandate. Any serious national strategy has to reckon with that, rather than hoping the market will get there.

### 4. Three opportunities score highly *because* of an existing Bangladeshi asset that has never been scaled
- **#8 Health purchasing** — SSK's own evaluation already shows a 92% out-of-pocket reduction among inpatient users, in Bangladesh, on Bangladeshi patients, and it covers 400,000 people.
- **#29 Foundational literacy** — BRAC has the delivery capability that most countries attempting TaRL lack.
- **#42 Blood supply** — Sandhani, Quantum and Badhan have the donor base; only the connective infrastructure is missing.

In each case the correct framing is not "import a foreign model" but "**finish something Bangladesh already started**".

### 5. The lowest-scoring items are not bad ideas — they are slow ones
Applied engineering colleges (#32, rank 48) and the regulatory guillotine (#9, rank 50) score low mainly on market potential and feasibility, not on merit. Both are 2035 opportunities whose payoff lands outside the window this matrix weights. A country planning to 2040 should treat the bottom of this table with more respect than the score implies — which is exactly why file 13's 2035 map is organised differently from this ranking.

## What the ranking deliberately does **not** say

- It does not say Tier C items should be ignored. Tier C contains the three opportunities with the strongest *causal* evidence in the entire dossier (drone logistics #37, lay-counsellor mental health #39, and TaRL-adjacent structured pedagogy), all of which scored down on market size rather than on whether they work.
- It does not adjust for who is asking. A government reader should re-run this with feasibility weighted lower and impact higher; a venture investor should weight feasibility, financial sustainability and competitive advantage to 20% each, which promotes #36, #17, #43 and #44 and demotes #2, #8 and #11 sharply.
- It does not price execution risk at the level of a specific team, which in practice dominates all ten criteria.
